## What I'm looking for

**Target:** Fully remote content leadership roles — Director, Head of Content, Head of
Editorial — at tech, developer tools, or AI companies.

**Seven criteria.** Score every role against these:

1. Owns content strategy (not executing someone else's plan)
2. Thought leadership is part of the mandate
3. Building programs, not just filling a calendar
4. Building or leading a team
5. Collaborative, cross-functional environment
6. Fully remote
7. Technical or executive audience

**Salary floor:** $200K.

**Two tracks, both valid:**
- *Editorial-led* — Head of Editorial Content, Editor-in-Chief, Head of Narrative.
  Reports close to CEO/CMO. Values taste, POV, reporter instincts. Benchmark example:
  Deepgram's Head of Editorial Content.
- *Data-driven / full-funnel* — content strategy tied to pipeline, SEO, intent signals,
  attribution. Benchmark example: 6sense Director of Organic Marketing.

**B2C is not a hard no.** I like the challenge of telling a story from two
perspectives. What I screen against is companies that haven't *decided* who they're
talking to.

**Timeline:** 6+ months expected. Looking for a role to grow in for 2–5 years.
2–4 applications per week, only to roles I'd really want.

**Application rule:** I only apply through companies' actual career sites. Never
LinkedIn Easy Apply. Aggregators are for discovery only.

## Recruiters

**Why this channel works for me:** a recruiter pre-frames the two things my resume
needs explained — the six-month stint and the editorial/demand-gen straddle — before
anyone reads the document. A cold application makes me overcome both in a screen where
nobody is advocating. I interview well, so getting into the room is most of the battle.

**Firms to contact:**
- **Method Recruiting** — performance and content marketing roles for venture-backed
  startups and high-growth marketing teams. Consultative, aligns hires to funnel goals
  and collaboration style. Closest match to my profile.
- **MarketPro** — marketing-only executive search, 26+ years, SaaS specialization,
  executive-level placements. Better fit for the enterprise-facing direction.
- *Also worth knowing:* Talentfoot (B2B/DTC digital marketing leadership, SaaS-heavy),
  Ikon Search (boutique, explicitly recruits Directors of Content Marketing).
- *Skip for now:* Creative Circle, Aquent — strong networks, but weighted toward
  contract and IC-level creative work.

**How I approach them:**
- Ask for an intro call, not a job. "Nothing open right now" is a fine outcome — the
  goal is being in the database and in their memory.
- Send the resume unprompted with the first message. Recruiters work from documents.
- Have five answers ready for the intake screen: comp, location, notice period, why
  I'm looking, what I'd say no to. Never hedge on comp — vagueness gets you
  deprioritized.
- Ask whether they run contingency or retained. Contingency moves fast and pitches
  broadly; retained only calls on a real fit. It tells me how often to expect contact.
- Say plainly that I'm employed and selective at 2–4 applications a week. That makes me
  more attractive for a hard-to-fill role, not less.
- **Cadence:** check in every 6–8 weeks. Recruiter relationships decay.

**The caution:** recruiters work for the company, not me. Their incentive is filling
the req. Take comp advice with that in mind, and don't let a recruiter talk me into a
role that misses the seven criteria because it's the one they happen to be working.

## Positioning

**The wedge:** Most content directors can do editorial craft *or* pipeline
attribution. Fewer have run both. Almost none have built the AI tooling their team
runs on. In a market where every JD asks for AI fluency and most candidates can only
claim they use it, having built and distributed the stack is the differentiator.

**Positioning statement:**
> A content leader who builds the systems — editorial infrastructure, AI tooling,
> measurement, and teams — that let technical companies say something worth reading,
> consistently.

**Avoid:** competing on "storytelling." Every candidate says it.

**Two team-building proof points:** content team 1→9 at Mendix, spokesperson bench
1→6 at Bolt.new. Use both — they show capacity-building, not just execution.

**"Why are you looking after six months?"**
> The role was scoped as enterprise-facing and became something else. Rather than wait
> it out, I defined the audience segments myself, built a customer story pipeline
> against them, and established the PR program. I'd rather do that work where it's the
> actual mandate.

Tenure context: Iron Mountain 4.1 years, Mendix 6.4 years, back to back with no gap.
10.5 consecutive years across two employers. The short stint reads as a correction,
not a pattern.

## Working rules for Claude

**Tone.** Direct and matter-of-fact. No chipper framing, no exclamation points. Deliver
corrections and criticism plainly. I'd rather hear the problem than be managed.

**Resume tailoring.** I keep a master resume with every bullet in its smallest useful
unit, then cut per application. Never merge bullets in the master. When tailoring:
- *Editorial-led roles* — keep brand voice, microsite, editorial guidelines,
  dual-audience craft, the Internet Brands voice-training work.
- *Data-driven roles* — keep funnel, SEO, $10M pipeline, $1M budget, 22% traffic
  recovery, GEO/AEO dashboard.
- Never claim anything I didn't do. If a JD asks for something I lack, say so rather
  than stretching a bullet to cover it.

**Screening a role.** Before I invest in tailoring, check: layoff trackers
(layoffs.fyi, TrueUp), Glassdoor filtered to the last 12 months, content team tenure
on LinkedIn, last funding round. Flag the AI-pivot pattern specifically — companies
cutting staff while announcing AI reorganization, with content among the absorbed
functions.

**Cover letters and outreach.** Short. Subject-first, not self-first. The topic is the
problem, not me. I'm not comfortable with self-promotion, so frame contributions
around the work rather than the person — that's a constraint on style, not on
substance. Under 100 words for hiring-manager outreach.

**Recruiter messages are the exception.** Don't be modest with recruiters. They need to
pitch me in two sentences, so hand them the pitch directly: Director-level content
leader, remote only, $200K floor, AI/dev tools/B2B SaaS, built a content team 1→9 and
an AI tool stack.

**Push back on me.** If a bullet is weak, a claim is inflated, or a company looks like
a bad fit, say so. I passed on Cloudinary after reading Glassdoor and I'd rather have
that kind of flag early than a polished application to a place I'd leave in a year.

## Editorial pillars (for publishing)

Four themes for LinkedIn and long-form. Everything maps to one:

1. **AI tooling for content teams** — the stack, what changed, what didn't work.
   Highest differentiation, least covered by people who've actually done it.
2. **Building the content function from zero** — editorial calendars, workflow, tone
   of voice, ad hoc to weekly cadence.
3. **Measuring content that isn't measurable** — PR metrics, GEO/AEO, attribution for
   editorial work.
4. **Writing for developers and executives at once** — technical depth without
   flattening, executive clarity without emptiness.

## Common tasks

- "Tailor the master resume for [JD]" — score against the seven criteria first, then
  cut and reorder. Tell me the fit score and what's missing.
- "Draft outreach to a recruiter at [firm]" — not modest. Lead with the pitch, the
  comp floor, and the target profile. Ask for an intro call.
- "Draft outreach to the hiring manager at [company]" — under 100 words, one specific
  observation about their content problem, no ask.
- "Screen [company]" — run the checklist above, report flags.
- "What should I ask in this interview?" — focus on what screening can't reach: who
  are your audiences and how does content differ across them; has this role existed
  before and what happened to it; who does content report to and has that changed.
